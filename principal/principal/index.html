C01 · Travel · Dimensional modeling

The Pricing Mart That Made Every Number Explainable

Reference blueprint: revenue-management decisions grounded in a consistent hotel and rate-plan grain.

Evidence boundary. Proposed travel-industry pattern for the 2006–2011 chapter. Validate client, role, architecture and outcomes before converting this to a confirmed case.

Business problem & revenue at stake

Concept: occupancy, ADR and RevPAR decisions fail when reservations, inventory and rate plans use incompatible grains.

Constraints

Era-correct reference stack: Oracle or Teradata, Informatica, Cognos/BO; nightly batch; late-arriving bookings and changing property hierarchies.

Key decisions & trade-offs

Reference choice: declare the booking-night grain and use Type 2 history for material commercial attributes; reject a single wide snapshot that hides change.

The hard part

Reference challenge: reconcile cancellations and rebookings without double-counting room nights.

Implementation & risk

Proposed pattern: parallel totals, property-level reconciliation and phased report cutover with rollback to the certified legacy extract.

Results

No historical outcome is claimed. Candidate measures: forecast error range, reconciliation variance, pricing decision latency.

Leverage & lesson

Reference leverage: a shared calendar, property and channel model. I would design source contracts earlier today.

C11 · EA · Platform control plane

Make Title Onboarding Configuration, Not Construction

Confirmed case subject: metadata-driven ingestion and shared controls for game telemetry.

Confirmed. EA telemetry flows through S3, Iceberg on EMR/PySpark, Snowflake and dbt; GCS/BigQuery pathways also feed Snowflake; Airflow orchestrates. Quantitative claims require validation.

Problem & revenue at stake

Each title brings different telemetry and domain context. Rebuilding quality, deployment and observability per title slows the analytics that support player experience and live operations.

Constraints

Multi-cloud sources, high-volume telemetry, title autonomy, evolving schemas and safe onboarding.

Decisions & trade-offs

Separate metadata control from runtime execution; standardize repeated controls while keeping domain semantics with title teams. Avoid a central team becoming the bottleneck.

The hard part

Design contracts strict enough to protect downstream products but flexible enough for game teams with different event lifecycles.

Implementation & risk

Reference implementations, validation gates and incremental title onboarding. Confirm the actual rollback and exception path.

Results

Candidate record from the prior portfolio, not yet public-ready: onboarding from four two-week sprints to one; 25+ titles / approximate platform scale.

Leverage

Shared ingestion, contract, quality and deployment patterns reduce repeated engineering across titles.

What I would change

Push contract feedback even closer to event creation and expose unit economics per title earlier.

What it taught me → the open table layer must carry trustworthy operational signals, leading to C12 and C13.

Confirmed scope

C12 · EA · Iceberg modernization

Open the Lakehouse Without Reopening Risk

Confirmed case subject: Apache Iceberg modernization and Snowflake interoperability.

Confirmed. Iceberg, EMR/PySpark, S3 and Snowflake interoperability are part of the supplied EA stack. Object counts and savings remain to verify.

Problem & revenue at stake

Legacy external-table patterns can constrain performance, lifecycle operations and engine optionality, raising the cost of trusted analytics.

Constraints

Large telemetry history, concurrent readers, schema evolution, snapshot lifecycle, safe migration and workload economics.

Decisions & trade-offs

Use an open table format and codify repeatable migration patterns. Balance file sizing, partition evolution and compaction cost; keep rollback through snapshots and dual reads during transition.

The hard part

Metadata and small-file behavior can erase theoretical gains. Partitioning must follow access patterns without recreating warehouse coupling.

Implementation & risk

Inventory access patterns, migrate in cohorts, compare query and cost baselines, then cut consumers over with explicit exit criteria.

Results

Candidate prior-portfolio figures to validate: 100+ objects and 40–50% lower query time and compute cost.

Leverage & lesson

Reusable table design, maintenance and migration patterns. I changed my mind: an open format does not create optionality unless the operating model is equally portable.

What it taught me → platform health must be visible across data, infrastructure and cost, leading to C13.

C13 · EA · Observability

Trust as a Platform Signal

Confirmed current architecture initiative: unified data quality, lineage, cost and infrastructure observability.

Confirmed boundary. The user is architecting this platform. It is not described as fully delivered, and no outcome metric is claimed.

Problem & revenue at stake

Data quality, lineage, spend and infrastructure health are often diagnosed in separate tools. That fragments ownership and lengthens recovery.

Constraints

Snowflake, Iceberg, dbt, Airflow and cloud infrastructure expose different telemetry and ownership models.

Decisions & trade-offs

Proposed direction: a shared metadata and incident model rather than a monolithic replacement for every specialist tool. Prefer explainable routing before autonomous remediation.

The hard part

Normalize assets, jobs, tests, owners and costs without losing the native identifiers needed for diagnosis.

Implementation & risk

Architecture in progress: start with common identity and high-value failure journeys; add agentic triage only behind evidence, confidence and approval boundaries.

Results

No result claimed. Validation targets: MTTD / MTTR, coverage, cost per product, false-positive rate.

Leverage & lesson

A common health model can make reliability and FinOps part of design review. The principle: contracts at the source, tests through the path, ownership at the failure.

What it teaches next → AI should act on certified context, not guess from raw telemetry.

Confirmed scope + concepts

C14 · EA · Semantic AI & ML foundations

Teach the Platform the Meaning Before the Language

Confirmed work areas: semantic models, conversational analytics patterns, ML feature foundations and AI enablement since Oct 2026.

Evidence boundary. Semantic and ML platform scope is confirmed. LLM text-to-SQL, cached tool interfaces and agentic quality patterns are architecture concepts unless separately validated as production deployments.

Problem & revenue at stake

Natural-language access amplifies inconsistency if metrics differ across titles. Personalization also fails when training and scoring features drift.

Constraints

Cross-title semantics, access controls, evaluation, hallucination risk, point-in-time correctness and clear model/platform ownership.

Decisions & trade-offs

Put certified semantic contracts before AI interfaces. Keep Data Science ownership of models and platform ownership of reliable data and feature contracts. Reject direct text-to-SQL over arbitrary raw tables.

The hard part

Meaning changes by title and time. Evaluation must test metric correctness, policy compliance and useful refusal—not just fluent SQL.

Implementation & risk

Start with bounded certified questions, versioned semantics, cached tools and traceable evaluation. Expand AI enablement patterns only with clear ownership.

Results

Prior-portfolio candidates to validate: 80+ analysts / 200+ questions per day, two semantic models, up to 20% campaign revenue lift linked to personalization models. Do not publish until sourced and scoped.

Leverage & lesson

Reusable semantic, feature and evaluation patterns for game teams. I was wrong to think access was the main bottleneck; shared meaning is.

What it taught me → trustworthy AI is an architecture of boundaries, semantics and evaluation.

02 / Architecture principles

Opinions earned the hard way.

These are the defaults I bring to ambiguous systems. Each can be reversed—when the evidence is stronger than the habit.

01
Model the business before the pipeline.

A fast wrong grain only moves disagreement downstream.

02
Contracts at the source, not tests at the end.

Downstream tests detect damage; good contracts narrow the blast radius.

03
Open formats need an open operating model.

Portability is hollow if metadata, policy and maintenance are engine-bound.

04
Replay is a product feature.

Streams without deterministic recovery are merely fast incidents.

05
Every byte has a cost owner.

Unit economics belong beside latency and quality in design reviews.

06
Centralize the repeated pain.

Keep domain meaning local; absorb undifferentiated controls into the platform.

07
AI earns scope through evaluation.

Fluency is not correctness. Expand autonomy only when failures are measured.

08
Migration needs an exit contract.

Every dual run needs parity criteria, rollback and a date for deleting the old path.
